Skip bin size guide

Skips are measured in cubic metres (m³). One cubic metre is roughly four standard 240-litre wheelie bins. Exact dimensions vary between operators, so treat this as a guide and ask when you get your quote. If you're unsure, it's usually cheaper to go one size up than to book a second bin.

2 m³

Mini skip

About 8 wheelie bins. Good for a garage or shed clean-out, a small garden tidy-up or clearing a few rooms. Also handy for small amounts of heavy waste like soil or broken pavers, which fill a bin's weight limit quickly.

3 m³

Small skip

About 12 wheelie bins. Suits a decent backyard clean-up, a single room clear-out or pruning after a big weekend in the garden.

4 m³

Bathroom or laundry reno

About 16 wheelie bins. A popular all-rounder for bathroom or laundry renovations, old carpet and underlay, or a small house clean-out.

6 m³

Kitchen reno or big clean-up

About 24 wheelie bins. Fits old cabinets and benchtops, furniture from a few rooms, or a larger yard clean-up with branches and garden waste.

8 m³

House clear-out

About 32 wheelie bins. Suits moving house, a deceased estate clean-out, or a medium renovation with mixed building waste.

10–12 m³

Building and big jobs

About 40–48 wheelie bins. Used for building sites, demolition of sheds or decks, and large commercial clean-ups. These need good truck access.

Skip bags of around 1 m³ are also available from some operators for very small jobs and tight spaces. Heavy waste (soil, concrete, bricks, tiles) usually has to go in a smaller bin because of road weight limits, even though it doesn't take up much space.

What can and can't go in a skip

Waste type matters as much as size. Operators sort and tip loads at licensed facilities, and each type of waste costs a different amount to dispose of. Being honest about what's going in gets you the right price and avoids surprise fees.

Usually accepted

  • General household waste: furniture, toys, clothes, boxes, cupboard contents and general junk.
  • Green waste: branches, leaves, lawn clippings, palm fronds and hedge trimmings (often cheaper on its own, with no dirt or rubbish mixed in).
  • Renovation waste: timber, plasterboard, old cabinets, carpet, tiles and fittings.
  • Heavy or clean fill: soil, sand, concrete, bricks, rock and pavers (often needs its own bin and has a lower weight limit).
  • Scrap metal: old roofing, fencing, frames and outdoor furniture.

Not accepted, or extra charge

  • Asbestos and fibro: never in a normal skip (see below).
  • Tyres: usually banned or charged per tyre.
  • Car and household batteries: a fire risk in a skip. Take them to a recycling drop-off instead.
  • Gas bottles and aerosols: can explode when compacted.
  • Paint, oils, chemicals and liquids: including pool chemicals and pesticides.
  • Mattresses: often accepted for an extra fee.
  • Food waste, medical waste and e-waste: check first. Many operators won't take TVs, computers or fluorescent tubes.

A note on asbestos

Many older Shoalhaven homes and sheds contain fibro, and it can contain asbestos. In NSW, asbestos must be wrapped, handled and disposed of under strict rules, and removing more than 10 square metres of bonded asbestos needs a licensed removalist. See SafeWork NSW's asbestos guidance. Some skip operators supply special asbestos bags or bins and transport them to an approved facility, but they won't remove it for you. If you suspect asbestos, say so when you ask for a quote.

For paint, gas bottles, batteries and household chemicals, check Shoalhaven City Council for local drop-off options and the NSW EPA for Household Chemical CleanOut events.

Putting a skip on the street: Shoalhaven City Council rules

The easiest option is to put the skip on your own property: your driveway, front yard or a clear patch of lawn. A bin on private land doesn't need Council approval. Lay a couple of planks under it if you're worried about marking your driveway, and make sure the truck can get in and out without hitting gutters, wires or trees.

If the skip has to go on the road or footpath, it's a different story. Under section 68 of the Local Government Act 1993, placing a waste storage container in a public place generally needs approval from Council unless an exemption applies. Shoalhaven City Council sets its own exemptions and conditions through its Local Approvals Policy, so the rules for your street may differ from other councils.

Before you book a road or footpath placement, check with Shoalhaven City Council about whether you need approval, how to apply, the fees and how long it takes. You can contact Council on 1300 293 111, by email at council@shoalhaven.nsw.gov.au or through the Shoalhaven City Council website. Allow time for this, and mention it when you request a quote, because your skip operator may be able to advise on placement.

Wherever it goes, keep the bin clear of driveways, hydrants, bus stops and pedestrian paths, and don't overfill it. Rubbish above the rim can't legally be carried on the truck.

Skip bin hire FAQ

How much does skip bin hire cost in Nowra?

It depends on the skip size, the type of waste, how heavy the load is, how long you keep the bin and where you are in the Shoalhaven. Heavy waste like soil, concrete and bricks costs more to tip than light household rubbish. Send us your details and we'll get you a firm quote from a local operator.

What size skip bin do I need?

As a rough guide, a 2 to 3 cubic metre skip suits a garage or garden clean-up, a 4 cubic metre skip suits a bathroom renovation or a small house clear-out, and 6 to 10 cubic metres suits a kitchen renovation, a full house clean-out or a building job. If you're unsure, tell us what you're throwing out and we'll help you choose.

Do I need a council permit to put a skip bin on the street in Nowra?

Placing a waste container on a public road or footpath generally needs approval from Shoalhaven City Council under section 68 of the Local Government Act 1993, unless an exemption applies. Check with Shoalhaven City Council on 1300 293 111 before you book a road placement. A bin placed on your own driveway or lawn does not need this approval.

Can I put asbestos in a skip bin?

Not in a normal skip. Asbestos has to be wrapped, handled and disposed of under strict NSW rules, and removing more than 10 square metres of bonded asbestos needs a licensed removalist. Some operators offer special asbestos bags or bins, so mention it when you ask for a quote.

How long can I keep the skip?

Most operators include a standard hire period of several days, and you can usually extend it for an extra daily fee. Tell us your dates when you ask for a quote so the price covers the time you need.

Do I need to be home for delivery?

Usually not, as long as the driver can reach the spot you want the bin and the area is clear. Leave clear instructions, and mention steep driveways, low wires, overhanging trees or narrow access when you book.
